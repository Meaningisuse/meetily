# P4 D1 diarization feasibility gate

**Decision: NO-GO for D3–D5.** Continue D2 timing work independently. Do not add a diarization runtime/model to the app until the listed gates are met. This is based on a one-sample model diagnostic, not a full held-out evaluation.

## Probe host

- OS: macOS 27.0.1, arm64.
- Hardware: MacBook Pro `Mac17,2`, Apple M5, 10 CPU cores, 24 GB unified memory, 10-core Apple GPU, Metal supported.
- Meetily's local `models/parakeet` directory is empty; no sherpa-onnx diarization CLI, segmentation model, or speaker embedding model was found in the app model directory.
- The repository contains no human-labeled Chinese diarization holdout. The existing `jfk.wav` fixture is not a multi-speaker Chinese meeting and has no RTTM annotation. A private AliMeeting Test evaluation set is prepared outside this checkout and is not committed.

## Candidate evidence

The sherpa-onnx official documentation exposes offline speaker diarization APIs and identifies separate segmentation and embedding model releases. Its Rust example pairs `sherpa-onnx-pyannote-segmentation-3-0` with `3dspeaker_speech_eres2net_base_sv_zh-cn_3dspeaker_16k.onnx`, and demonstrates Chinese audio. This establishes a plausible probe path, not measured quality, app integration, or package readiness: [sherpa diarization docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/speaker-diarization/index.html), [official Rust example](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/blob/master/rust-api-examples/examples/offline_speaker_diarization.rs).

The individual converted ONNX release artifacts' redistribution terms still need written source-level confirmation. The [3D-Speaker repository](https://github.com/modelscope/3D-Speaker) is Apache-2.0 and lists its pretrained ERes2Net models on ModelScope, but that code/repository license does not establish the exact license or conversion provenance of sherpa-onnx's separate ONNX release. A September 2026 sherpa-onnx discussion asks about precisely these artifacts; the maintainer directs the question to the original authors without confirming artifact redistribution terms: [model license discussion](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/discussions/3939). The alternative `pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1` model card declares CC BY 4.0 and gates access on accepting contact-information conditions: [model card](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1). Neither candidate is treated here as cleared for redistribution in the Meetily app bundle.

### Public Mandarin corpus candidates checked (2026-10-14)

Two potential labeled sources exist, so the evidence is no longer “no public Mandarin meeting corpus found”; neither has been downloaded or assembled into the project holdout:

- [AISHELL-4 on OpenSLR](https://www.openslr.org/111) is listed as CC BY-SA 4.0 and described as 120 hours across 211 recorded meetings with 4–8 speakers, overlap/noise/quick turns, transcriptions, and speaker activity labels. Its smallest listed training archive is still 14 GB. The AISHELL baseline repository's Apache-2.0 applies to its code; it must not be mistaken for the OpenSLR corpus license.
- [AliMeeting on OpenSLR](https://www.openslr.org/119) is also listed CC BY-SA 4.0 and described as 118.75 hours across 240 meetings with 2–4 speakers. The listed Eval archive is 3.42 GB and includes far-field and headset audio.

These corpora could supply Mandarin multi-speaker research material under their published data license with attribution and ShareAlike obligations. They do not by themselves cover a real one-speaker meeting, code-switching, or all device/compression conditions. The [pyannote segmentation model card](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/segmentation-3.0) says the model used the training sets of AISHELL and AliMeeting. Their training partitions and corpus-level benchmark numbers are therefore in-domain evidence, not an untouched holdout. OpenSLR lists separate AliMeeting Train/Eval/Test partitions (212/8/20 sessions); its Test partition is a candidate session-level holdout for a segmentation model trained on the published training partition, provided the frozen session manifest is checked against the exact model and embedding-model provenance before tuning. A read-only inventory of the official [`Test_Ali.tar.gz` archive](https://speech-lab-share-data.oss-cn-shanghai.aliyuncs.com/AliMeeting/openlr/Test_Ali.tar.gz) completed on 2026-10-14: the 9,555,497,666-byte archive passed `gzip -t` and contains 80 WAV files and 80 Praat TextGrid files. The inventory found 20 distinct meeting identifiers, 20 eight-channel 16 kHz far-field WAVs, 60 mono 16 kHz headset WAVs, and speaker tiers in the 20 far-field TextGrids; the longest track per meeting totals approximately 10.78 hours. `prepare_alimeeting_test.py` now pairs meeting-level TextGrids with far-field audio, anonymously remaps labels, and prepares a private mono 16 kHz set. It produced 20 samples / 646.591 minutes; audio and RTTM, private manifest, and CC BY-SA 4.0 attribution remain outside Git. The first sample contains four labeled speakers and 866 reference turns. This is a viable public-data holdout candidate, but the full session/model-overlap audit and minimum-size DER run remain outstanding. Supplemental one-speaker/code-switch samples are still required. Separately, the official [Rev Reverb model license](https://huggingface.co/Revai/reverb-diarization-v1/blob/main/LICENSE) limits use to testing/research/personal/evaluation in Non-Production Environments and expressly excludes internal employee usage in an employer's commercial business activities; this candidate is unsuitable for Meetily's stated internal business use. The sherpa-onnx Pyannote release archive includes a MIT notice and identifies the converted segmentation model's source. The 3D-Speaker upstream ModelScope card declares Apache-2.0 for ERes2Net; keep that notice with the candidate, and do not treat a runtime release file by itself as proof that the complete model bundle is cleared for product redistribution.

The Go gate remains NO-GO. The 19-session minimum-size holdout was run with a frozen speaker-count-guided configuration, but the reference-derived speaker counts make it an optimistic upper bound; its 37.94% micro DER is well above the 20% gate. Results were notably poor for 3- and 4-speaker meetings (56.10% and 48.48% group micro DER); the 2-speaker group reached 22.56%, still above the gate. The full session/model-training overlap audit, one-speaker and code-switch samples, 120-minute single-recording resource test, cancellation, packaging, and complete artifact-license evidence remain missing. Threshold tuning on the development meeting did not rescue the unsupervised mode. This sherpa candidate should not proceed to D3–D5; evaluate a different diarization model or segmentation/embedding pipeline before another held-out run. Benchmark numbers from model cards or upstream demo WAVs cannot replace this project's held-out Chinese meeting evaluation.

Input-channel follow-up: on the first development meeting, sherpa with an oracle 4-speaker count scored 17.08% DER / 24.89% overlap DER on the prepared 8-channel average-downmix. Using raw channel 0 alone scored 34.19% / 37.39%. This rejects the simple single-channel-0 substitution for this sample; it does not evaluate beamforming or other channel combinations.

## Reproducibility artifacts

`scripts/diarization-probe/prepare_alimeeting_test.py` converts the official archive to anonymous private WAV/RTTM files with attribution. `scripts/diarization-probe/run_probe.py` runs the external sherpa CLI against a WAV plus RTTM and produces a private JSON report with hardware, model hashes, version, DER/overlap DER, RTF, peak RSS, license/access notes, and model bytes. It intentionally does not download data or models. The score mapper supports more predicted clusters than reference speakers and counts unmatched predicted clusters as false alarms. Private audio, RTTM, model weights, and reports remain outside Git; only anonymous aggregate evidence may be committed.

The probe was smoke-tested with a generated 10-second silent WAV, synthetic RTTM, placeholder model files, and a fake CLI that returns matching turns. This checks argument wiring, report serialization, and DER plumbing only; it is not diarization, model, performance, cancellation, or quality evidence.

To change this decision, prepare and authorize the minimum set described in [`frontend/src-tauri/tests/fixtures/diarization/README.md`](../frontend/src-tauri/tests/fixtures/diarization/README.md), make model/runtime versions reproducible, verify each weight's terms and packaging rights, and run the probe on the holdout plus a separate 120-minute load sample. Until then, D3–D5 remain paused by the plan's explicit gate.

## D2 timing status (2026-10-14)

**Status: source mapping, precision persistence, and guarded Whisper segment-time propagation are implemented; real-model timing quality and performance remain unverified.** The `audio::time_map` helper maps half-open intervals from packed 16 kHz samples to original source sample spans. It splits output at source discontinuities and rejects unmapped or overlapping packed ranges. Absolute sample-rate conversion uses one rational operation to avoid per-chunk rounding drift.

The focused Rust tests pass for a VAD gap (packed 1–2 s maps to source 10–11 s), an interval crossing that gap (returned as two spans), 44.1/48 kHz conversion over 120 minutes, and invalid/unmapped ranges. These tests verify mapping arithmetic only.

Parakeet has no provider timing and continues to return `unavailable` with no timed segments. Whisper preserves the established no-timestamp decode as canonical text and runs a second timestamp-enabled decode; it emits segment times only if the texts match after limited CJK whitespace and terminal sentence-punctuation normalization and all centisecond ranges are ordered and within audio duration. A failed or mismatched second pass keeps canonical text and returns unavailable timing. Recording, catch-up, import, and retranscription carry verified segments into persisted rows; coarse chunk/VAD bounds remain approximate. Existing rows are backfilled by the migration using the same rule, and meeting documents expose precision in schema version 2. Focused tests cover timestamp conversion/validation, incomplete tails, English word boundaries, punctuation-only differences, failure fallback, precision propagation, recording expansion, legacy-data backfill, and document readback. The full Rust suite currently passes 326 tests (4 ignored); the focused timing helper has 6 tests.

A model-backed smoke regression also ran through `WhisperEngine::transcribe_audio_with_verified_timing` with a generated 11.033-second Tingting Chinese TTS sample and multilingual `ggml-tiny.bin` (SHA-256 `be07e048e1e599ad46341c8d2a135645097a538221678b7acdd1b1919c6e1b21`). Both CPU and Apple M5 Metal runs completed the canonical and timestamp-enabled passes. The two passes retained the same final spoken phrase; their only difference was a trailing Chinese full stop. The guard accepted the segment at 0–10,900 ms and kept the exact canonical text for display. Tiny misrecognized “会议纪要” as “会议技药”, so this proves text-tail preservation across the two decodes only, not Chinese transcription accuracy or timestamp accuracy against annotation. The existing JFK Metal smoke example also completed on Apple M5. On macOS, reproduce the TTS fixture with `say -v Tingting -r 165 -o /private/tmp/chinese-tail.aiff '我们今天讨论项目安排。首先确认需求，然后安排负责人。最后，请记得周五之前发送更新后的会议纪要。'`, then `/opt/homebrew/bin/ffmpeg -y -i /private/tmp/chinese-tail.aiff -ac 1 -ar 16000 -c:a pcm_s16le /private/tmp/chinese-tail.wav`; download the model from `https://huggingface.co/ggerganov/whisper.cpp/resolve/main/ggml-tiny.bin` and verify the SHA-256 above. Set `WHISPER_TIMING_TEST_MODEL` and `WHISPER_TIMING_TEST_AUDIO`, then run `cargo test --locked -p meetily --lib timestamp_decode_preserves_synthetic_chinese_tail -- --ignored --nocapture` from `frontend/src-tauri`. Add `WHISPER_TIMING_USE_METAL=1` to enable Metal; this mode requires a process environment where Metal devices are visible.

The prior SIGSEGV was isolated to the restricted test sandbox: `MTLCreateSystemDefaultDevice()` returned `nil` there, while a read-only probe outside the sandbox enumerated Apple M5. The whisper.cpp Metal path then loaded the model and completed inference when run outside the sandbox. This is an environment limitation, not evidence of a Meetily Metal defect. A CPU probe used the public AliMeeting Test sample: with `ggml-tiny`, the first 120 seconds and eight 30-second speech-rich windows all completed the dual-decode test, but every window safely returned `Unavailable` because canonical and timestamp-pass text differed. A same-window `ggml-base` run also returned `Unavailable`. The logs and clips remain outside Git. These runs verify fallback on real Chinese meeting audio, not timestamp accuracy. Three-entry playback and comparison of emitted exact segments against independent word/time annotation remain unverified. D2 therefore remains incomplete, and no real-meeting or diarization-quality claim is made.
